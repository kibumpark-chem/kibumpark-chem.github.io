About

I develop computational methods at the intersection of chemistry, physics, and computer science to understand how atoms and molecules interact. A central focus of my research is understanding how proteins navigate their energy landscapes as they fold into their native structures—a process that can sometimes fail. This provides a concrete setting for studying broader questions about molecular structure, dynamics, and interactions. I am particularly interested in the physical principles underlying protein folding and in developing molecular simulation tools and force fields that deepen our understanding of its atomistic basis.

I use molecular simulation, force-field development, and machine learning to connect molecular mechanisms with the larger spatial and temporal scales relevant to biology. Experimental data inform and validate these methods, while simulations provide molecular-level detail and mechanistic insight that experiments may not directly resolve. I develop methods and software to make these models more accurate, efficient, and reliable.

I view computation as part of an iterative feedback loop in which increasingly data-rich experiments and physical theory guide the development and evaluation of molecular models and simulation methods, while simulations and machine learning generate new hypotheses and reveal where those models can be improved. This perspective motivates my interest in approaches that combine the interpretability and physical grounding of mechanistic models with the flexibility and scalability of modern data-driven methods.

Looking ahead, I aim to deepen our understanding of the physical principles governing protein structure, dynamics, and function while contributing to practical advances in protein and small-molecule therapeutics. In particular, I am interested in building computational tools that are accurate, robust, and interpretable, and capable of addressing genuinely challenging problems in protein biophysics and drug discovery.

Research

I work on both sides of the same problem: the physical principles that govern how proteins behave, and the computational tools needed to study them at a useful scale.

  • Protein Folding

    Protein Biophysics & Computational Biology

    I study how protein sequence shapes folding landscapes and how protein biogenesis modulates the pathways by which proteins reach, maintain, or recover their native states. By characterizing the dynamics and energetics of folding, I aim to investigate how folding constraints contribute to sequence variation and the evolution of protein function. This perspective provides a biophysical complement to sequence-based analyses and may enable deeper interpretation of metagenomic diversity beyond conventional alignment-based approaches.

    Read more →

  • Conformational Sampling & Energy Models

    Method & software development

    Studying a folding landscape reliably takes two things: an energy function that ranks structures correctly, and sampling that can actually reach them. I work on both, and on the software that makes them usable. Machine learning enters on both sides: in the knowledge-based potential, and in the Monte Carlo move set.
