About

I'm a PhD candidate in Chemistry and Chemical Biology at Harvard, working with Eugene Shakhnovich to understand the physics of protein folding: why some proteins fold on their own, and why others depend on molecular chaperones to reach their native structure. My work combines molecular simulation with machine learning, from comparing folding landscapes across species to structure prediction with AlphaFold. Before Harvard, I completed a First-Class Honours Master of Chemistry at the University of Oxford. I also organize the Greater Boston Area Theoretical Chemistry Seminar Series, bringing in speakers from BU, MIT, and Harvard.

Research

  • Doctoral Researcher, Harvard University

    2020–Present

    In the Department of Chemistry and Chemical Biology, advised by Prof. Eugene Shakhnovich.

    • Compared folding landscapes of chaperone-dependent and independent protein homologs (actin, eEF1A, Rli1) to identify what separates proteins that fold on their own from those that need help.
    • Identified topological traps, such as non-native contacts and hydrophobic core expansion, that lock proteins into misfolding states they can't escape without assistance.
    • Simulated cotranslational folding of the SARS-CoV-2 receptor-binding domain, showing how disulfide bond timing during synthesis steers the protein toward its native structure.
    • Improved AlphaFold3 structure prediction for a disease-linked protein complex by combining it with phylogeny-aware sequence alignments, raising interface confidence from 0.67 to 0.80.

  • Computational Chemistry Research Intern, Genesis Therapeutics

    Jun–Aug 2024

    Burlingame, CA

    • Built an OpenMM-based workflow combining grand canonical and non-equilibrium Monte Carlo moves with molecular dynamics simulations, to better sample protein-ligand binding and water networks for detecting cryptic pockets and alternate binding modes.

  • Undergraduate Researcher, University of Oxford

    2018–2020

    Department of Chemistry, advised by Prof. Brianna Heazlewood.

    • Built computational models of molecular reaction rates at ultracold conditions, combining classical and quantum mechanical methods to extract kinetic parameters.
