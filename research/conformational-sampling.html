Method & software development

Conformational Sampling & Energy Models

Studying a folding landscape reliably takes two things: an energy function that ranks structures correctly, and sampling that can actually reach them. I work on both, and on the software that makes them usable. Machine learning enters on both sides: in the knowledge-based potential, and in the Monte Carlo move set.

Knowledge-based force fields

I am developing knowledge-based force fields that use experimental data to improve the description of protein energetics. Many current approaches rely on evolutionary information from multiple sequence alignments, which is powerful but less effective for individual point mutations that are poorly represented in the available data. My work instead incorporates experimentally measured information directly into the energy model.

  • Developing experimentally informed approaches for improving knowledge-based protein force fields.
  • Studying how force fields can better represent the energetic effects of point mutations.
  • Curating benchmark decoy sets that test whether a model can distinguish native and near-native structures from non-native decoys.

Monte Carlo simulation

I am developing pyMCPU, a Monte Carlo suite for protein simulation, built with protein folding, misfolding, and conformational landscapes in mind. Its Python and C++ components support efficient sampling, rapid prototyping, and the development of new simulation methods.

  • Building a Python and C++ framework for Monte Carlo simulation of proteins.
  • Developing cache-efficient data structures for fast sampling of protein energy landscapes.
  • Supporting rapid testing of new collective variables and sampling methods.

Code

  • PhyloMSA — phylogeny-aware MSA pipeline for protein–protein complex prediction in co-folding models
  • pyMCPU — Monte Carlo suite for protein simulation; public release in preparation
