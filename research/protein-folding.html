Protein Biophysics & Computational Biology

Protein Folding & Quality Control

I study how proteins navigate complex folding landscapes and how cells help them avoid, resolve, or recover from non-native states. My work centers on two connected questions: how molecular chaperones reshape folding pathways, and how proteins fold cotranslationally as they are being synthesized.

Chaperone-assisted folding

I compare chaperone-dependent and chaperone-independent homologs to find the physical features that separate them. Using molecular simulation, I investigate how non-native contacts, chain topology, and hydrophobic-core formation create kinetic traps and reshape the folding landscape.

  • Compared folding landscapes of chaperone-dependent and -independent homologs, including actin, eEF1A, and Rli1.
  • Identified topological traps involving non-native contacts and hydrophobic-core expansion that can stabilize misfolded states.
  • Studied how chaperone systems alter the energetic and kinetic accessibility of productive folding pathways.

Co-translational folding

Folding does not always wait for synthesis to finish. I study how folding begins during translation, and how the timing of chemical events such as disulfide-bond formation guides a nascent chain toward its native structure.

  • Simulated co-translational folding of the SARS-CoV-2 receptor-binding domain.
  • Investigated how the timing of disulfide-bond formation influences the folding pathway and final structure.
  • Studied the role of ribosome-associated chaperones in vectorial folding of nascent proteins.
